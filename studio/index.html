The wood

Every branch comes from one ranch in Descanso, in the mountains east of San Diego. I walk the property and select by hand. Nothing is cut that does not need to come down, and the landowner signs off on every load.

Ember is cut while the tree is living. Bone is standing deadwood that the sun has already bleached. Same species, two completely different objects.

The hands

I spent twenty years producing events in San Diego. Ballrooms, backyards, hotel lobbies at two in the morning. I was always the one asking the florist for one more branch, then moving it myself after everyone left. At some point I realized the branch was the part I loved.

So now I make the object. I pick the wood, I sand it until it is smooth enough to run a hand down, I build the box it stands in. When a planner calls for fifteen matching pieces, I know exactly what arrives broken and what photographs, because I have been on the other end of that call.

Dense wood rings. Listen.

Six seconds. A Bone branch, tapped twice.
